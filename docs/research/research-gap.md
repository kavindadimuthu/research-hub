# Research Gap and Problem Statement

## Where the literature falls short

The passenger demand forecasting literature is extensive and growing. High-resource settings — Paris, Hong Kong, Tokyo, New York — have well-documented forecasting frameworks, large-scale benchmark datasets, and multiple competing approaches whose relative merits are well understood.

Sri Lanka occupies a fundamentally different position. Two gaps stand out:

**Gap 1 — No generalisable forecasting framework for Sri Lanka.**
No published work has combined multiple Sri Lankan data sources (ticket sales, GPS/AVL traces, weather, calendar events) at the route or stop level to produce a demand forecasting model. Existing Sri Lankan studies examine individual data streams in isolation and do not produce comparative, reproducible forecasting results.

**Gap 2 — Rural and inter-provincial corridors are neglected.**
The small body of data-driven Sri Lankan transport studies focuses almost exclusively on the Colombo metropolitan area. Rural and inter-provincial corridors — which carry significant ridership and face distinct demand patterns — are largely absent from the research record.

---

## Why these gaps matter

Without accurate forecasts of *when and where* passengers travel, evidence-based planning is not possible. Operators cannot:

- Match fleet size and frequency to actual demand patterns
- Anticipate peak and off-peak demand shifts
- Respond to seasonal variation (school terms, festivals, monsoon season)
- Make the case to regulators for route or schedule changes backed by data

The consequence is the status quo: overcrowded peaks, empty off-peak services, and schedules that persist by inertia rather than evidence.

---

## Statement of the problem

Operators in Sri Lanka — both SLTB and private — currently lack a reliable, fine-grained understanding of when and where passengers travel, because operational data is fragmented and analytically underused. Without such an understanding, evidence-based planning is not possible.

The problem this research addresses is:

> **How can heterogeneous Sri Lankan transport data be combined and modelled to produce accurate short- and long-term passenger demand forecasts at the route and stop level?**

---

## Scope of the contribution

This project addresses both identified gaps by:

1. Building the first multi-source demand forecasting framework tested on Sri Lankan corridors
2. Covering urban, suburban, and inter-provincial settings — not just Colombo

:::note What this project does NOT claim to do
Designing mechanisms that translate forecasts into accessibility outcomes (dashboards, planning workflow integration, policy interventions) is explicitly out of scope. The deliverable is a rigorously evaluated forecasting model. Downstream applications are recommended future work.
:::
