# Sri Lanka Transit Demand Forecasting

**IS4101 Final Year Research Project — University of Colombo School of Computing (UCSC)**

> *A Data-Driven Approach to Forecasting Passenger Demand for Improved Public Transport Accessibility in Sri Lanka*

---

## What this research is about

Public transport is a critical pillar of urban mobility, economic productivity, and social equity in Sri Lanka. Services operated by the Sri Lanka Transport Board (SLTB) alongside private operators carry the majority of daily commuters — particularly low- and middle-income households who do not own private vehicles.

Despite this, the system suffers from chronic inefficiencies: overcrowded peak-hour services, underutilised off-peak routes, unreliable schedules, and limited information about when, where, and in what numbers passengers actually travel.

The root cause is that operational decisions — route planning, fleet allocation, frequency setting — continue to rely on infrequent manual surveys and operator experience rather than systematic analysis of demand data. Sri Lanka has only recently begun to digitise fare collection and vehicle tracking, and the analytical use of these emerging datasets remains largely unexplored.

**This research builds the predictive foundation that evidence-based planning requires.** By developing and rigorously evaluating a demand forecasting framework tailored to the Sri Lankan context, the project provides the capability on which subsequent accessibility-improvement work can build.

---

## The deliverable

A **developed and evaluated passenger demand forecasting model** that:

- Ingests heterogeneous data sources (ticketing, GPS/AVL, weather, calendar, census)
- Produces accurate short-term (hourly/daily) and long-term (weekly+) forecasts
- Is evaluated on both local Sri Lankan corridors and publicly available global transit benchmarks
- Is documented with full methodology, feature engineering choices, model selection rationale, and error analysis
